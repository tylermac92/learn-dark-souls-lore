Drangleic: The Same Story, Much Later

Dark Souls II never tells you outright how it connects to the first game. It leaves clues in the mouths of shopkeepers and prisoners, and you have to piece them together.

Your win today: place Dark Souls II on the arc. When it happens, where it happens, and what has stayed the same. And show that a DS2 character tells Lesson 4's story in his own words.

Warm-up · mixed eras

Quizzes are harder from now on. Questions mix eras, “who said it?” asks you to match quotes to their source, and select-all questions give you no credit for eliminating options.

When: long after, and the same curse

DS2 opens with a new narrator, but the brand is the same one:

You will lose everything, once branded. The symbol of the curse, an augur of darkness. … By then, you will be something other than human. A thing that feeds on souls, a hollow.

Long ago, in a walled off land far to the north. A great king built a great kingdom. I believe they called it Drangleic. …

Like a moth drawn to a flame, your wings will burn in anguish. Time, after time. For that is your fate. The fate of the cursed.

Dark Souls II, opening narration
Transcription from the Fextralife DS2 Lore page.

Compare it with Lesson 1. There, the Undead were “corralled and led to the north.” Here, the cursed are drawn to “a walled off land far to the north.” The curse, the Hollowing and the pull northward are all the same. What has changed is the scale: “time, after time.”

Where: a land that keeps being rebuilt

Three DS2 characters and one ring describe the ground under Drangleic:

Many Kingdoms rose and fell on this tract of Earth. Mine was by no means the first. … But then, from the ashes, a flame reignites, and a new king is born, sporting a new face.

Straid of Olaphis, Lost Bastille

Since long, long ago, many kingdoms have risen and fallen in this very spot. Maybe that's why most people don't remember much about the past.

Stone Trader Chloanne

A ring used long, long ago in a land that existed where Drangleic does now.

Ring of the Lingering Dragoncrest, DS2 item description
All three quoted in J.C. Wigriff's Total Lore and Location Breakdown (Fextralife). The Lingering Dragoncrest Ring is also a DS1 item.

The text says three things: kingdoms have been built on this spot again and again, people have forgotten them, and an older land once stood here. Many lore writers go further and say Drangleic stands where Lordran once stood, Wigriff among them. That is a reading. It's plausible, since you're in the far north and a DS1 ring turns up, but no DS2 text names Lordran. Even Fextralife's own lore page leaves the question open.

What stayed the same: the cycle

Straid's line has the whole pattern in one sentence: “from the ashes, a flame reignites, and a new king is born.” Sweet Shalquoir, the cat in Majula, says it too: “Everything will crumble and waste away so that something new may be born.” DS1 gave you one choice at the Kiln. DS2 shows you that choice has been made many times, in many kingdoms.

Shalquoir quoted in the same Wigriff breakdown.

The old story, told again

Aldia, Scholar of the First Sin, says this to you at the Undead Crypt bonfire:

Once, the Lord of Light banished Dark, and all that stemmed from humanity. And men assumed a fleeting form. These are the roots of our world. Men are props on the stage of life, and no matter how tender, how exquisite... A lie will remain a lie.

Aldia, Undead Crypt
Dialogue from the Fextralife Aldia page.

Set that beside Lesson 4. Kaathe said Gwyn “blurred your past.” The Ringed Knight Armor says the gods sealed early men with fire. Aldia, in a different game, says the Lord of Light banished Dark and everything that came from humanity, and that the human form you wear is “fleeting,” even “a lie.” That's a third source, from a third game, describing the same act. (“Lord of Light” = Gwyn is itself a reading, but an easy one. Gwyn is the Lord of Sunlight.)

The shape to remember

DS2 = the cycle seen from far away. Much later, in the far north, on ground where kingdom after kingdom has risen and fallen. The curse is unchanged. The fire is still fading and still being rekindled by new kings. And at least one person, Aldia, knows the original sin underneath it all.

Primary source · read this

J.C. Wigriff, “Total Lore and Location Breakdown” on the Fextralife DS2 wiki. It's the most thorough text-first walk through DS2, and it quotes its sources.
