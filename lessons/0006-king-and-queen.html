Lesson 6 · Era IV · About 12 minutes

The King and the Queen

Drangleic's king built a great kingdom, went to war, and then walked into a crypt and never came out. His queen is still on the throne, waiting for you.

Your win today: tell the story of Vendrick and Nashandra, tie Nashandra back to a DS1 boss, and explain Vendrick's idea about fire and Dark. You'll also handle something new: two versions of the game that disagree with each other.

Warm-up · Lesson 5 again, plus older eras

The queen: a piece of the Abyss

If you collect the King's Crown and the DLC crowns, Vendrick tells you what his wife was:

One of the Father of the Abyss' spawn, that confounded quintessence of humanity. The Abyss once had form, but then dissipated. And yet, traces of its existence endured. Each fragment, thirsting for power, spread Dark, with no relent.

My dear Shandra… was one such fragment… A feeble, tiny thing that thirsted for power more than any other… Driven by insatiable lust for a worthy vessel.

Vendrick (later dialogue, after the King's Crown and two DLC crowns)
Dialogue from the Fextralife Vendrick page.

The “Father of the Abyss” is a title you already know from DS1's Artorias of the Abyss DLC: the boss Manus, Father of the Abyss. So: Nashandra is a fragment of Manus. Strictly that's a reading, since Vendrick never says “Manus,” but it's a short one: one title matched to one boss name.

Her own soul (in the original release) explains how fragments like her live:

The fragments of the abyss, of untold origin, nourished their beings by the sides of would-be monarchs. Perhaps they were simply ordeals on the way to kinghood. One day, the flames will fade, and only darkness will remain. Unless, of course, an heir arrives.

Soul of Nashandra, original DS2 description
Soul of Nashandra on Fextralife, which also notes that Scholar of the First Sin cut this text.

And the Emerald Herald, at the end, says what she wants: “She covets the First Flame, and the Great Soul.” Nashandra attaches herself to a king, feeds on him, and waits for someone strong enough to reach the throne.

Herald line from the Fextralife Emerald Herald page.

The king: a war, then a crypt

King Vendrick, in adherence to Queen Nashandra's counsel, led his armies north in an effort to subjugate the Giants, and claim their powerful souls for his own.

Giant Warrior Club, item description

After the King retired to the Undead Crypt, Velstadt followed, never to return.

Soul of Velstadt

This great soul beckoned the Dark, overwhelming the King. Perhaps this King, who once ruled the world of men, was a lacking vessel for the true throne.

Soul of the King, Scholar of the First Sin description
From the Fextralife pages for Giant Lord, Vendrick and Soul of the King.

Line them up: on his queen's advice, Vendrick goes to war for the Giants' souls. The war ends in ruin. He retires to the Undead Crypt with his loyal knight Velstadt, and when you find him he is Hollow, walking in circles. His great soul drew in the Dark, and he turned out to be a “lacking vessel.” Why he retreated is never stated. One common reading is that he understood what Nashandra was and denied her the vessel she wanted. Keep that one as a reading.

New skill · when the text disagrees with itself

Scholar of the First Sin (2015) rewrote some DS2 descriptions. The Giant Lord's soul originally said the Giants “landed on the northern shores, and set siege to King Vendrick's castle.” The rewrite says “Vendrick crossed the seas, took prisoner the Giants, and brought them back to his castle, clapped in irons.” So who started the war? The two versions disagree. Most readers take the later text, which matches the Giant Warrior Club, but you should know both exist. When someone quotes DS2 lore, ask which version they're quoting.

Both versions on the Fextralife Giant Lord Soul page.

Vendrick's idea

Vendrick's dialogue ends with the series' own creation myth, word for word, and then takes it somewhere new:

Fire came to be, and with it, Disparity. Heat and cold, life and death, Light and Dark. Dark was seen as a curse. Shadow is not cast, but born of fire. And, the brighter the flame, the deeper the shadow. Inherit fire, and harness the Dark. Such is the calling of a true leader…

Vendrick

Frampt said cast away the Dark, and Kaathe said embrace it. Vendrick says neither. Fire and Dark are one system, so the stronger you make the fire, the deeper the Dark it creates. A “true leader” would hold both. That third option is where Lesson 7 begins: Aldia and the Throne of Want.
