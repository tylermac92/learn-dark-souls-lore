Lesson 1 · Era I · About 10 minutes

Before the Fire, and the Four Who Found It

The whole trilogy is one long argument about a fire. This lesson gives you the first page of that story, told in the order the game tells it.

Your win today: narrate the creation myth of Dark Souls in five beats, from memory, in the right order. Every later lesson hangs off these five beats.

Warm-up

You said you know the basics. Before you read on, try this in your head: who were the four who found the Lord Souls, and who was the dragon who betrayed his own kind? Hold your answer. You'll check it below.

The source

Nearly everything in this lesson comes from one place: the three-minute narration that opens Dark Souls. It is the longest, most verbose cutscene in the game, and it is the closest thing the series has to an official creation myth.

On the cutscene's length and role: Walantus, GDC 2017 Narrative Review. Quotations below are from the transcription on the Fextralife Dark Souls Lore page.

The five beats

  1. The Age of Ancients

    In the Age of Ancients, the world was unformed, shrouded by fog. A land of gray crags, Archtrees and Everlasting Dragons.

    No time, no change, no death. The dragons are everlasting, and that is the point: nothing ends because nothing is different from anything else.

  2. Fire brings Disparity

    But then there was fire, and with fire came disparity. Heat and cold, life and death, and of course, light and dark.

    The First Flame creates opposites. Life now has death as its partner, light now has dark. Keep that last pair in mind. The trilogy's endings all turn on it.

  3. The Four find the Lord Souls

    Then from the dark, they came, and found the Souls of Lords within the flame.

    Four figures take a Lord Soul each:

    WhoHow the narration names them
    Nito“the first of the dead”
    The Witch of Izalith“and her Daughters of Chaos”
    Gwyn“the Lord of Sunlight, and his faithful knights”
    The Furtive Pygmy“so easily forgotten”

    Notice the small phrase: they came from the dark. Even Gwyn, the Lord of Sunlight, started there.

  4. The War against the Dragons

    Gwyn's mighty bolts peeled apart their stone scales. The Witches weaved great firestorms. Nito unleashed a miasma of death and disease. And Seath the Scaleless betrayed his own, and the Dragons were no more.

    Three Lords fight, and each brings their own power: lightning, fire, death. The Furtive Pygmy gets no line in the war at all. The narration called the Pygmy “easily forgotten” and then proves it. Hold that silence for Lesson 2.

    The dragons lose because one of them, Seath the Scaleless, turns on them. You fight him in the Duke's Archives.

  5. The Age of Fire, and its fading

    Thus began the Age of Fire. But soon, the flames will fade, and only Dark will remain.

    The narration jumps straight from triumph to decline. Now there are only embers. Among the living appear people branded with the Darksign, the mark of the Undead. They are rounded up and sent north to be locked away until the world ends. The narrator closes on you: “This is your fate.” That locked-away place is the Undead Asylum, where you start the game.

The shape to remember

Fog → Fire → Four → War → Fading. Sameness, then difference, then those who claim the difference, then their victory, then the cost. Dark Souls 2 and 3 replay the last beat over and over.

Practice

Answer from memory. Don't scroll up. Getting one wrong now helps more than getting it right by peeking.
