Lesson 3 · Era III · About 10 minutes

Two Serpents, One Choice

Dark Souls gives you two advisers, both ancient, both certain, and both pointing you at the same final door for opposite reasons.

Your win today: explain what Gwyn did to the First Flame, what each serpent wants you to do about it, and which choice gives each of DS1's two endings.

Warm-up

What Gwyn did

Kaathe told you Gwyn sacrificed himself to link the fire. The soul you take from Gwyn at the end of the game agrees:

Soul of Gwyn, the Lord of Sunlight and Cinder, who linked the First Flame.

Lord Gwyn bequeathed most of his power to the Gods, and burned as cinder for the First Flame, but even so, Lord Gwyn's soul is a powerful thing indeed.

Soul of Gwyn, Lord of Cinder, item description
Item text from the Dark Souls wikidot.

So linking the fire means a Lord giving himself to the First Flame as fuel. Gwyn gave his power away first, then burned. The figure you fight at the Kiln of the First Flame is what is left: the Lord of Sunlight has become the Lord of Cinder. Here two very different sources agree, which makes this one of the firmest facts in the series.

The first serpent · Frampt

When you ring both Bells of Awakening, Kingseeker Frampt rises at Firelink Shrine.

I am the Primordial Serpent, Kingseeker Frampt, close friend of the Great Lord Gwyn.

Your fate is… to succeed the Great Lord Gwyn. So that you may link the Fire, cast away the Dark, and undo the curse of the Undead.

Kingseeker Frampt, Firelink Shrine
Dialogue from the wikidot and Fextralife pages for Frampt.

He sends you to Anor Londo for the Lordvessel, whose own description backs him up:

Lordvessel bestowed upon the chosen Undead who is destined to succeed Lord Gwyn. … To open the final door, place this vessel on the Firelink Altar, and fill it with powerful souls.

Lordvessel, item description

Then he names the souls. They must be “commensurate to the great soul of Gwyn”:

Gravelord Nito, the Witch of Izalith, the Four Kings of New Londo, and Lord Gwyn's former confidant, Seath the Scaleless.

Kingseeker Frampt

Two of those are Lord Souls from Lesson 1. The Four Kings and Seath drop a Bequeathed Lord Soul Shard, “a fragment of a Lord Soul discovered at the dawn of the Age of Fire.” The standard reading is that these are pieces of Gwyn's own soul. That fits with Gwyn having “bequeathed most of his power,” but no item says it outright. Notice which soul is not on the list: the Dark Soul.

Shard text from Fextralife.

Both serpents need you to fill the Lordvessel, so both want the same thing from you right up to the final door. They only disagree about what you do once you're through it.

The choice at the Kiln

You kill Gwyn, Lord of Cinder. Then:

  • Light the bonfire → Linking the Fire. You succeed Gwyn as the Great Lord and do exactly what he did. Frampt's plan.
  • Walk away → the Dark Lord. The fire is left to fade, and you become the lord of an Age of Dark, the age of men. Kaathe's plan.
Ending triggers and outcomes as summarised on the Fextralife Endings page.
The shape to remember

Gwyn burned himself to keep the fire going. Frampt wants you to do the same. Kaathe wants you to let it die. Linking the fire doesn't end the problem. It only buys time, and the next game opens in the world that time bought. Dark Souls III's opening cinematic speaks of the “Lords of Cinder,” the same title Gwyn's soul gives him.

DS3 opening line quoted by PC Gamer.
