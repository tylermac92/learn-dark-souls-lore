Lesson 4 · Era III · About 10 minutes

What the Curse Is

You died hundreds of times across three games. This lesson is about why the world let you come back, and what it cost.

Your win today: explain the Undead curse as a mechanism with three parts (the Darksign, Hollowing and humanity), and give the leading reading of why it exists. Part of the answer is hidden in Dark Souls III's last DLC.

Warm-up

Part 1 · The Darksign brings you back

The Darksign signifies an accursed Undead. Those branded with it are reborn after death, but will one day lose their mind and go Hollow.

Death triggers the Darksign, which returns its bearer to the last bonfire rested at, but at the cost of all humanity and souls.

Darksign, item description (DS1)
DS1 text from the Dark Souls wikidot.

Read it as a rulebook. The Darksign revives you, but every death takes your humanity, and the end state is Hollow: a mind that has gone. Your respawn, the bloodstain you run back to and the Hollows you kill all come from this one item description.

Part 2 · Hollowing and humanity pull against each other

The Crestfallen Warrior at Firelink Shrine treats humanity as the thing that keeps you from Hollowing:

Oh, your face! You're practically Hollow. … Hm, what? Restoring your humanity? … the quickest way, although I'd never do it, is to kill a healthy Undead, and pillage its humanity.

Crestfallen Warrior, Firelink Shrine
Dialogue from the wikidot Crestfallen Warrior page.

The game itself works this way too. Spend one humanity at a bonfire to reverse Hollowing and you look human again. Die, and the Darksign takes it all. So the curse is a tug of war: the Darksign drains humanity, humanity holds off Hollowing. And humanity, from Lesson 2, is your dark inheritance from the Pygmy.

Part 3 · Why does the curse exist?

Frampt gave you the first clue in Lesson 3: linking the fire will “undo the curse of the Undead.” So the curse is tied to the fire fading. The rest of the answer turned up years later, in Dark Souls III's final DLC, The Ringed City:

The armor of early men was forged in the Abyss, and betrays a smidgen of life. For this reason the gods cast a seal of fire upon such armor, and those who possessed them.

Ringed Knight Armor, item description (DS3: The Ringed City)

This was nothing less than an attempt to reveal that which the seal of fire has occluded, a subtle defiance of the gods' wishes.

Ringed Knight Hood, item description
DS3 text from Fextralife: Ringed Knight Armor, Ringed Knight Hood.

And the Dark Sigil, which Yoel of Londor gives you in DS3, describes the brand from the other side:

A black gaping hole in the flesh that resembles the brand of an Undead. The darkness of humanity seeps from this bottomless pitch-black hole, the gap filled by the accumulation of the curse.

Dark Sigil, item description (DS3)
Dark Sigil on Fextralife.
The leading reading

The gods put a seal of fire on humans to hold down their dark nature. The Darksign is that seal, a ring of fire around a dark hole. While the First Flame burns strongly, the seal holds. As it fades, the seal fails and the brand shows: humans become Undead. That is why linking the fire “undoes” the curse, and why Kaathe says Gwyn “blurred your past.”

This is a reading, built from DS1 dialogue and DS3 DLC text that came out six years apart. It's the most widely held answer, but no single line in any game states it.

One more connection: the DS3 Darksign repeats the DS1 rules (reborn, eventually Hollow) and adds one line: “And so it is they are driven from their homeland.” That is the same fate as the Undead “corralled and led to the north” in Lesson 1's narration. The curse plays out the same way in every age.

DS3 Darksign on Fextralife.

Practice

Primary source · read this

Read the three Darksign-related texts one after another: Darksign (DS1), Darksign (DS3), Dark Sigil (DS3). Then the Ringed Knight Armor. It takes five minutes, and you'll see the reading take shape from the text yourself.
